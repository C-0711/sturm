# Big Media: few conglomerates, almost everything

<p class="lead">In 1983, 50 corporations controlled 90% of US media. Today: 5–6 conglomerates — Comcast, Disney, Paramount, Warner Bros. Discovery, Fox. How much concentration is too much — and what the facts do (and do not) prove about the "government is captured" thesis.</p>

## Summary

US media consolidated from 50 companies (1983, Ben Bagdikian's "Media Monopoly") to 5–6 conglomerates spanning broadcast, streaming, cinema and much of the internet. Concentration is a documented fact (FCC data, corporate reports). The political conclusion — "therefore all news is coordinated" — is a non sequitur. Concentration structurally means fewer people decide over more topics; it does not prove top-down control. Separate the two and you have a strong argument; merge them and you lose it.

## Debunking

**False:** "Everything is controlled from the top." — Concentration is a structural problem. Direct control would need case-by-case evidence (see the revolving-door dossier). Claiming it without evidence is conspiracy theory.

**Also false:** "Concentration doesn't matter, the market sorts it out." — 5–6 companies over 90% is not a functioning market. The question is not whether but how much concentration democracy can bear.
