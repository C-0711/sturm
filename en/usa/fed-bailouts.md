# The Fed: $16 trillion in emergency loans — and who uncovered it

<p class="lead">During the 2007–2010 financial crisis the Fed cumulatively lent **$16 trillion** to banks at home and abroad — in secret. Revealed not by Congress but by a one-time GAO audit (forced by Dodd-Frank) and Bloomberg's FOIA lawsuit. What this says about the state: it is not governed by "the people" alone — its institutions fight their own battles.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Document</span> GAO-11-696 (July 2011) <span class="tag red">Amount</span> $16T cumulative</div>

## Summary

The GAO audit (GAO-11-696, July 2011), mandated by Dodd-Frank § 987, documented the Fed's emergency programs 2007–2010: $16 trillion in cumulative lending to domestic and foreign banks, some at near-zero rates. The Fed resisted disclosure for years; Bloomberg won the recipient details by FOIA suit (2010–2011). Congress never established a recurring audit duty — the one-time audit stayed one-time. The case evidences how minimal parliamentary control over central-bank emergency policy really is.

## Debunking

**False:** "The Fed saved the state." — The Fed kept the banking system liquid; whether that "saved the state" is normative. The evidence shows money flowed to banks while millions lost their homes.

**Also false:** "The Rothschilds control the Fed." — Conspiracy myth. The documented problem is lack of control, not a secret family.
