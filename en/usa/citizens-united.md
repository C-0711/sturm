# Citizens United: when corporations became speakers

<p class="lead">In 2010 the Supreme Court held 5–4: corporations and unions may spend without limit on independent political advertising. Since then: Super PACs and "dark money". The decision that defined money as speech and made transparency optional.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Case</span> 558 U.S. 310 (2010) <span class="tag red">Result</span> 5–4: independent spending protected</div>

## Summary

Citizens United v. FEC (21 January 2010) struck limits on independent expenditures by corporations and unions: as long as spending is uncoordinated with a campaign, it is protected "speech". Corporations now fund Super PACs running attack ads; "dark money" (via 501(c)(4) groups that hide donors) grew into a billion-dollar industry.

## Debunking

**False:** "Citizens United allows direct donations to candidates." — No: **independent** expenditures only. Direct corporate contributions remain banned (Tillman Act 1907). The distinction is technical — and it is the core of the debate.

**Also false:** "Dark money is only Republican." — Both parties use 501(c)(4)s and Super PACs. The abolition of transparency is bipartisan.
