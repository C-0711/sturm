# Murthy v. Missouri: the government on the phone with the platforms

<p class="lead">Two states and five users sued dozens of officials: the government pressured platforms into censorship. In June 2024 the Supreme Court dismissed the case — for lack of standing. The merits were never decided.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Case</span> 603 U.S. ___ (2024) <span class="tag red">Result</span> 6–3: no standing, no merits decision</div>

## What happened

Between 2020 and 2022 the White House, CDC, FBI and other agencies communicated routinely with Facebook, Twitter/X, YouTube and Google about moderating content — mostly Covid-19. The plaintiffs (including Missouri and Louisiana) sought an injunction. A district court granted a sweeping one.

## The ruling (26.06.2024)

The Supreme Court vacated: no plaintiff could trace a **specific** moderation act by a **specific** official at a **specific** platform to **their own** speech. The majority (Barrett) acknowledged the record shows the government "played a role in at least some of the platforms' moderation choices". Alito, dissenting with Thomas and Gorsuch: the Court "erects an impenetrable wall around the Government".

## Debunking

**False:** "The Supreme Court confirmed there was no censorship." — Wrong. It dismissed on standing; the documented communications are real and uncontradicted.

**Also false:** "The court proved state censorship." — It did the opposite of decide. Both spins are wrong; the record is the evidence.
