# Browser &amp; search: the first step to self-determination

<p class="lead">The browser is your window on the world; search is your map. Whoever controls both controls what you see. Three steps, no dogma.</p>

## Browser

**Recommendation: Firefox or LibreWolf.** Independent engine (Gecko, not Chromium), configurable tracking protection, container tabs for identity separation. Chrome is the market king — and a Google product.

| Browser | Engine | Google-free | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firefox | Gecko | Yes | Strong (ETP, total cookie protection) |
| LibreWolf | Gecko | Yes | Hardened |
| Brave | Chromium | Mostly | Hardened |
| Chrome | Chromium | **No** | Marketing "private" — practice: data linkage |

**Steps:** install Firefox → set default → uBlock Origin → optionally `privacy.resistFingerprinting` for the hard setup.

## Search

**Recommendation: Startpage or SearXNG.** Startpage serves Google results without your IP/history going to Google. SearXNG is self-hostable, aggregating many sources. DuckDuckGo is the middle way (Bing results, less collection — but not tracking-free; the 2022 browser contract scandal showed Microsoft ties).

**The real point:** the question isn't only "who collects data?" but "whose index?" Only an own index (Mojeek, marginal) is truly independent of Google/Bing.

## Debunking

**False:** "Chrome incognito is private." — Incognito hides history from other users of the device, not from Google, websites, or your ISP.
