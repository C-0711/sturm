# Identity: pseudonyms are a right

<p class="lead">"Real names" policies are control instruments. Pseudonymity — a persistent identity without a legal name — is the foundation of free expression and legally protected in Germany and Europe. What holds, what's coming, what to do.</p>

## The law (documented)

- **Germany:** pseudonymity is part of free expression (Art. 5 Basic Law), confirmed by the Federal Constitutional Court; § 3 DDG-E allows anonymous use of digital services where possible.
- **EU:** the DSA imposes no real-names duty on platforms; Art. 14 strengthens minors' protection without identity disclosure for adults.
- **Conversely:** the Chat Control debate shows the right to pseudonymity stands and falls with the privacy of communication itself.

## What you can do

1. **Separate identities:** one public (name), one private (pseudonym), one throwaway — never the same email/phone for all.
2. **Own your email:** your own domain (€10/year) + mailbox.org or self-hosted. Whoever owns their address switches providers without losing everything.
3. **Purpose aliases:** SimpleLogin/addy.io addresses per service — when one leaks, you know who sold you out.

## Debunking

**False:** "If you have nothing to hide, you need no pseudonym." — The argument confuses guilt with freedom: pseudonymity protects the innocent — whistleblowers, stalking victims, people under repressive regimes, and anyone who wants to err without being defined by it forever.

**Also false:** "Anonymity = impunity." — Anonymity is not immunity: prosecution still has its tools, by court order. Pseudonymity makes communication lawful, not lawless — it protects everyday life from the "we know who you are" reflex.
