# The COMPACT ban: a ministry bans a magazine, a court stops it

<p class="lead">July 2024: Germany's Interior Ministry bans the magazine Compact, seizing its assets. Two years later the Federal Administrative Court declares the ban unlawful — the magazine keeps publishing.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Country</span> Germany <span class="tag">Topic</span> Association ban, press freedom <span class="tag red">Status</span> Ban voided</div>

## Summary

On 16 July 2024 Interior Minister Nancy Faeser (SPD) banned the far-right magazine Compact under § 3 of the Associations Act, with asset seizure and raids in several states. On 24 June 2025 the Federal Administrative Court (BVerwG) voided the ban as **unlawful**: a ban achieved via association law may not effectively prohibit what press freedom otherwise allows. The court expressly noted the magazine's rhetoric exceeds the danger threshold — but that does not justify banning a press enterprise.

## Debunking

**False:** "The court acquitted Compact." — No. It voided the ban on formal grounds (press enterprise), while confirming the rhetoric is hostile to human dignity. Both facts matter; neither alone is the story.

**Also false:** "Germany doesn't censor — the ruling proves it." — The ruling proves a ministry *tried* to shut a magazine and the courts stopped it. Both the attempt and the check are the evidence.
