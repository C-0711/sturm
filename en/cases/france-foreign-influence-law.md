# The anti-influence law: a title that doesn't match its contents

<p class="lead">The 2024 law "against foreign interference" was meant to stop foreign disinformation. The Conseil d'État itself complained before adoption: the bill goes beyond foreign influence and should honestly be titled "strengthening the protection of democratic life". What passed — and what critics fear.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Country</span> France <span class="tag">Topic</span> Disinformation law <span class="tag red">Status</span> In force since 26.07.2024</div>

## Summary

Loi n° 2024-850 of 25 July 2024 (following the Nunez report) expands powers against manipulation campaigns — formally against "foreign" actors. The Conseil d'État's opinion on the bill stated it does not only cover foreign interference and suggested renaming it "law to strengthen the protection of democratic life". Civil liberties groups and RSF warn that the definitions could reach domestic critical reporting — depending on who defines "manipulation". Implementation decrees are still pending.

## Debunking

**False:** "France passed a censorship law against disinformation." — Precisely: a law against **foreign** interference whose definitions, per the Conseil d'État, exceed its title. Whether it becomes a censorship tool depends on practice that doesn't yet exist.
