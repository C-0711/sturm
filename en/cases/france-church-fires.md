# Church Fires in France: What Is Actually Burning

<p class="lead">Viral claim: "400 churches burned to the ground!" Reality: France's Interior Ministry counts 843 anti-Christian acts in 2025, up 9% — and arson is rising across Europe. A dossier about real numbers, invented numbers — and why both go viral.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Country</span> France <span class="tag">Topic</span> Religious freedom, disinformation <span class="tag red">Status</span> Numbers verified · viral claims debunked</div>

## Summary

France is experiencing a real, measurable rise in anti-Christian crime: the Interior Ministry (["Actes antireligieux — Tendances 2025"](https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse/actes-antireligieux-tendances-2025)) recorded **843 anti-Christian acts in 2025 — a 9% increase over 2024**; 87% target property (including fires), 13% target people, with personal attacks up 70%.[^1] OIDAC Europe (Observatory on Intolerance and Discrimination against Christians) documented **2,211 anti-Christian hate crimes across Europe in 2024, including 94 arson attacks on churches — nearly double 2023** — one third (33) in Germany; the highest counts come from France, the UK, Germany, Spain and Austria.[^2]

At the same time, a viral claim has circulated for years that "400+ churches were burned" in France (and globally). The BBC's *More or Less* ([episode of 06 June 2026](https://www.bbc.com/audio/play/w3ct998s)) fact-checked that number — it does not hold.[^3] Reuters showed in 2024 that the widely shared "fires map" of France actually plots **all** alleged crimes against churches, not just fires.[^4]

**The finding fits both failure modes of our century:** inflating the real numbers with panic ("400 burned!") makes the real trend incredible. Trimming the real numbers makes it invisible. Both are disinformation — just with opposite signs.

## Evidence

- [Ministère de l'Intérieur: "Actes antireligieux — Tendances 2025"](https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse/actes-antireligieux-tendances-2025) — 843 anti-Christian acts in 2025 (+9%), 87% against property, 13% against people (+70%), 34% of all anti-religious acts.[^1]
- [OIDAC Europe: "Intolerance and Discrimination Against Christians in Europe — Report 2025"](https://www.intoleranceagainstchristians.eu/publications/oidac-report-2025) — 2,211 anti-Christian hate crimes in 2024 (own documentation: 516; including theft/burglary: 1,503), **94 arson attacks**, nearly double 2023; focus countries include France; background: the near-destruction of the historic church of Saint-Omer (09/2024).[^2]
- [BBC More or Less, 06 June 2026: "Factchecking claims that 400 churches were burned to the ground in France"](https://www.bbc.com/audio/play/w3ct998s) — review of the viral 400-number for France, the US and Canada.[^3]
- [Reuters Fact Check, 25 July 2024](https://www.reuters.com/fact-check/map-shows-all-alleged-crimes-against-churches-france-not-just-fires-2024-07-25/) — the map shows all alleged crimes against French churches, not just fires.[^4]
- [Premier Christianity](https://www.premierchristianity.com/news-analysis/churches-are-burning-across-europe-but-why/18575.article) — arson context: OSCE data 2018–2022 lists 102 church arsons classified as hate crimes; 2022/23 rose from 60 to 105; "over 200 arson incidents affecting churches in the last five years" (church insurer spokesperson); France and Italy: 16 hate-arson cases each in 2022/23.[^5]

## Clearing up the lies

**False 1:** "45 churches burned in France / around 50 arson attacks on churches." — This figure appears in no official statistic and no traceable source. What is documented: **94 church arsons across Europe in 2024** (OIDAC), 33 of them in Germany — for France 2025, **no arson count is published** in the cited ministry balance. Precise-sounding numbers without sources, in either direction, turn a real trend into noise.

**False 2:** "Anti-Christian acts rose by 86%." — The ministry reports **+9%** (843 acts in 2025 vs 2024). The 86% figure circulates on social media; no ministry document supports it. **What is true:** +70% among the (smaller) personal-attacks category, and +88% for **anti-Muslim** acts (326 cases). Confusing these three numbers doubles the drama — measurably.

**False 3:** "400+ churches were burned." — Checked by the BBC and not supported; the shared map plots all crimes against churches.[^3][^4] The real number (94 arsons, Europe, 2024) is large enough — quadrupling it is not needed to raise concern.

**False 4 (opposite direction):** "Anti-Christian crime is a myth." — False: 843 officially recorded acts, one murdered Christian in 2025, a nearly burned-down church in Saint-Omer 2024, 94 arsons across Europe. The trend is real — only the viral magnitudes are not.

## Open questions

- France announced an **annual public report** on anti-religious acts for the first time in 2026.[^1] Will it publish per-country arson figures — or will they stay buried in aggregate categories?
- The ministry's own numbers are explicitly **not institutional statistics** ("Ces données chiffrées ne constituent pas une statistique institutionnelle") but trend data.[^1] How large is the dark figure among congregations that never report minor attacks?
- OIDAC triangulates its own documentation with official statistics to avoid double counting — three sources with three definitions. Which number wins in a dispute?
