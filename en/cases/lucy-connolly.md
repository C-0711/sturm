# Lucy Connolly: 31 months in prison for a tweet

<p class="lead">On the day of the Southport stabbing (29 July 2024) the British childminder Lucy Connolly posted on X: "Mass deportation now, set fire to all the hotels…". She was jailed for 31 months. Her appeal was refused in May 2025.</p>

<div class="meta-line"><span class="tag">Country</span> UK <span class="tag">Topic</span> Public Order Act 1986 <span class="tag red">Status</span> Final; appeal refused</div>

## Summary

Connolly, 41, pleaded guilty to inciting racial hatred. Northampton Crown Court sentenced her to 31 months on 17 October 2024, citing the context of the Southport riots in which asylum hotels were actually attacked. The Court of Appeal refused leave in May 2025; she was released after serving roughly half.

## Debunking

**False:** "A tweet = 31 months, that's censorship." — The tweet called for burning asylum hotels during riots where hotels were attacked. The court weighed real danger, not just words. But the proportionality debate is legitimate: comparable violence cases often draw less.

**Also false:** "She was jailed for just one tweet." — She pleaded guilty to stirring up racial hatred; the sentencing context was the Southport unrest. Where the line sits is exactly the debate — in the UK, Germany, Brazil alike.
